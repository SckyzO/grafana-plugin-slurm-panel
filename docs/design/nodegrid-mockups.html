1 · Why the wall is not green

The obvious palette is a traffic light. Measured against the palette validator, it is the worst of the three options. Flip the vision switch above to protan or deutan and compare the columns: the middle one collapses.

paletteworst pair, deficient vision contrast on whiteverdict
green / amber / red ΔE 4.1 deutan amber 1.83:1 fail
Grafana defaults ΔE 6.2 protan green 2.24:1 · orange 2.15:1 fail
quiet OK ΔE 10.2 deutan · 13.9 tritan all ≥ 3:1 pass

No hue can fix a traffic light. Red against green measures ΔE 4.1; red against a desaturated slate measures 13.0. The separation comes from dropping chroma on the healthy state, not from picking a better green. It also fixes a second problem: on a healthy cluster 95% of cells are OK, and a loud green wall drowns the three red cells it exists to reveal. Healthy should be quiet.

One check is deliberately failed. The validator flags #3f7f96 as below the chroma floor — it “reads gray”. That floor stops a categorical series from being mistaken for a grid line. Here the quiet is the message, and UNKNOWN is separated by shape, not hue. Documented, not overlooked.

2 · The cell carries a second channel

The validator permits a palette in the ΔE 6–8 band only with a secondary encoding. Rather than treat that as a rule to satisfy, it is the one place this panel spends its boldness: the state changes the shape of the cell, not only its fill. The grid stays readable in greyscale, in print, under forced-colors, and across a room.

left to right: OK, WARN, CRIT, UNKNOWN — at 28px, 14px and 8px

Below 10px the notch stops being legible and the fill carries alone, which is also where a cell stops being a hover target. That is the honest floor of the density ladder in section 5, and the panel says so rather than shrinking past it.

3 · Rack, or wrap

The same 96 nodes, twice. Wrap fills the width and answers “how much of the cluster is broken”. Rack gives each group a fixed-width column read bottom-up, standing on a floor line, and answers “which cabinet do I walk to”. Same cell, same card, same link — only the arrangement differs.

4 · The group header is one line

A rail two pixels wide carries the rolled-up state. No card, no badge, no shadow, no radius — every pixel spent on chrome is a pixel not spent on a node. The header carries what cannot be inferred from the cells: the name, how many nodes, whether the grouping was assumed, and the tally.

20 nodes · 25 slots. A node in three partitions appears in three groups, so the two counts differ and both are shown. A count that quietly disagrees with sinfo is worse than no count.

5 · How far this scales

Four clusters, same cell size, same panel height. The only thing that changes is how much of the cluster fits on screen at once — which is the whole argument. By the last one a single grid is past the point of being worth reading, and the answer is to split the view by region. That is a product decision, not a rendering one.

6 · The node card

What a drained node's owner actually needs, in the order they need it. The reason is the headline, not a footnote — it is the single fact no aggregate can reach, and the whole reason this panel exists.

7 · When something is off

Nothing is ever painted healthy for lack of information. A node with no value is a hollow ring; a state the panel has not been taught is counted and named; a grouping the panel invented says so.
